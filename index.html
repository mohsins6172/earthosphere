    Great Resets

    The Five Mass Extinctions

    Five times, life on Earth nearly ended — and five times it rebuilt itself into something new. Each bar shows the share of species lost.

    The sixth? A chapter we are writing.

    Many scientists warn that human activity — habitat loss, climate change and pollution — is driving extinction rates far above the natural background. Unlike the first five, this one has a cause that can choose differently. Understanding the past four billion years is the first step toward writing a better next page.

    A Planet That Runs Hot & Cold

    Climate Through Time

    Earth has swung between scorching greenhouse ages and frozen "snowball" worlds. But look closely at the very end of the story — the change we are causing now is unlike anything in the record for its speed.

    Deep-time climate — the last 500 million years

    Global temperatureCO₂ (log scale)

    Warm "greenhouse" spells (like the age of dinosaurs) alternate with cold "icehouse" spells (like the ice ages we just left). Values are approximate.

    The modern spike — the last 170 years

    Temperature anomalyCO₂ (ppm)

    Since the Industrial Revolution, CO₂ has risen from ~280 to over 420 ppm and average temperatures ~1.2 °C — a jump that natural cycles took thousands of years to make. This is the "hockey stick".
